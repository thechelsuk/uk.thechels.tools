--- title: Cipher Encoder and Decoder permalink: /cipher category: text tags: [ciphers, puzzles, text] added: 2026-10-25 summary: Encode and decode text with Caesar, Atbash, Vigenère or Morse code. description: "Free cipher tool. Encrypt and decrypt messages with the Caesar shift, Atbash, Vigenère keyword cipher or Morse code, in your browser." scripts: [app.js] how_to: - Choose a cipher. Caesar needs a shift; Vigenère needs a keyword. - Type your message and press **Encode** or **Decode**. - For Morse, letters are separated by spaces and words by `/`. about: | These are the classic ciphers from puzzle books, escape rooms and school projects: - **Caesar** moves every letter along the alphabet by a fixed number. Julius Caesar is said to have used a shift of 3. - **Atbash** swaps A with Z, B with Y and so on. Encoding and decoding are the same step. - **Vigenère** uses a keyword to shift each letter by a different amount, which hides letter patterns far better than Caesar. Only letters use up the keyword, so spaces and punctuation don't change the result. - **Morse** turns letters and digits into dots and dashes. Results come back in capitals, since the ciphers work on the 26 letters. None of these are secure by modern standards; use them for fun, not secrets. faq: - q: How do I crack a Caesar cipher? a: Try decoding with every shift from 1 to 25. Only one will produce readable words. - q: Why do some characters come back as "?" in Morse? a: They're codes the decoder doesn't recognise, usually a typo. They're listed under the result. related: [/string-sorter, /escape-entities] ---
